Recent progress has been made \cite{alexandruIntrinsicallyCorrectAlgorithms2026} in formalising synergies between proofs of partial and total correctness of algorithms defined using structured recursion \cite{hinzeConjugateHylomorphismsMother2015}.
This was acheived with the introduction of the novel notion of a well founded functor.
We acheive the same result, i.e. a criterion for all coalgebras for an endofunctor on the power category \(𝒞^A\), for \(A\) the carrier of a well founded ordering \(<\), to be recursive.
Our criterion is a novel instantiation of the preexisting notion of a locally contractive functor \cite{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}.
This instantiation is acheived by enriching \(𝒞^A\) in presheaves over \((A,<)\) using change of base of enrichment along the cofree presheaf functor.
Our hope is that demonstrating how this preexisting notion can be instantiated for this novel application may lead to a broader adoption of the technique, as well as enabling reuse of all existing known results about locally contractive functors in this setting.

\paragraph{Recursive Coalgebras}
Expressing recursive algorithms using coalgebra-to-algebra morphisms has a long history \cite{CaprettaUV06,jeanninWellfoundedCoalgebrasRevisited2017,taylor99,meijerFunctionalProgrammingBananas1991}.
The key idea is the following: In a base category \(𝒞\) modeling the types and functions of your functional programming language, choose an endofunctor \(F \colon 𝒞 → 𝒞\). We can think of this functor as describing a distribution; e.g.\ the functor \(B X ≔ 1 + X × A × X\) describes the distribution of either a base case \(* : 1\) or two inputs \(l , r : X\) and an output \(o : A\).
A recursive algorithm \(h\) can be seen as the following sequence of steps:
\begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=~]
\item \emph{divide} input into new input, with a distribution given by \(F_0\);
\item recur under \(F_1\);
\item \emph{combine} the results of the recursive invocations.
\end{enumerate*}
E.g.\ the recursive case of Quicksort \autoref{fig:qsort-recursive} (due to \cite{CaprettaUV06}); the general case is \autoref{fig:coalg-alg-morphism}.

A coalgebra \(c\) is called \emph{recursive}, if for every target algebra \(a\) there is a unique \(h\) such that \eqref{fig:coalg-alg-morphism} commutes, i.e.\ the recursive equation \(h = c ; F h ; a\) has a unique solution and can be read as a \emph{definition}.
Intuitively, this means \(c\) is a division step that, when iterated, builds only well founded trees of shape \(F\), regardless of the starting input \(i : I\).
The challenge of defining an algorithm as a coalgebra-to-algebra morphism in a total language then can be reduced to proving the recursivity of its coalgebra.

This challenge becomes easier to tackle when we take partial correctness into account. Continuing with our Quicksort, we home in on its coalgebra, \textsf{partition}. Its type, \(\mathsf{List} A → 1 + \mathsf{List} A × \mathsf{List} A\) is really too coarse. The partial correctness of \textsf{partition} entails
\begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=~]
\item the output \(B (\mathsf{List} A)\) value contain the same \emph{elements} as the input;
\item the elements to the left/right of the selected pivot be respectively smaller/greater than it.
\end{enumerate*}
One approach to refine its type in order to capture this, due to \cite{AlexandruCRW25}, is to move to the setting of \emph{families} in \textsf{Set} indexed by the set \(ℳA\) of finite multisets of element type \(A\).
The refined \(B\) becomes \(\bar{B}\) (expressed for better comprehensibility in the slice category \(\mathsf{Set}/ℳA\) instead of in \(\mathsf{Set}^{ℳA}\)) :
\[
  \bar{B} \begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    f
  \end{pmatrix}
  ≔
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 \\
    * ↦ \emptyset
  \end{pmatrix}
  +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \{(l,p,r) ∈ X × A × X \mid ∀ e_l ∈ f(l), e_r ∈ f(r).\, e_l ≤ p ≤ e_r\} \\
    (l, p, r) ↦ f(l) \uplus \{p\} \uplus f(r)
  \end{pmatrix}
\]
The interesting thing to note is the following: The multiset indices in functorial positions are smaller than the result index: \(f(l),f(r) < f(l) \uplus \{p\} \uplus f(r)\).

\paragraph{Intro}\label{sec:intro}
In particular, that means we are able to define a functorial action of \(F\), \(F_1'\) that, intuitively, takes morphisms \(A → B\) at smaller indices and produces a morphism \(F_0 A → F_0 B\) at the current index, such that \(F_1'\) agrees with \(F_1\). This then allows us to essentially construct a solution to \eqref{fig:coalg-alg-morphism} as an inhabitant of the homset \(I → O\) using well founded recursion. We proceed to make all of these notions formal. For notions of enriched category theory we mostly follow~\cite{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}.

\paragraph{Enrichment}
\label{sec:enrichment}
To talk about an action of a functor \(F\) that isn't \(F_1\), we need the notion of:
\begin{definition}[Enrichment of a category \(𝒞\) in a monoidal category \(𝒱\), {\cite[Def.~2.2]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}}\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Base}{Enrichment}{}]
  An enrichment of a category \(𝒞\) in a monoidal category \((𝒱,⊗,I)\) consists of the following data:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item for every \(x, y \colon 𝒞\) the external Hom \(ℰ_{X,Y}\), an external identity morphism \& sequencing operation, and external counterparts to unitality \& associativity coherences;
  \item for every \(x, y \colon 𝒞\) a bijection \(\expi{-}\colon 𝒞(x,y) ≅ 𝒱[I,ℰ_{X,Y}]\) witnessing that \(𝒞\) and the underlying category with objects \(𝒞_0\) and homsets \(𝒱[I,ℰ_{X,Y}]\) of \(CE\) viewed as an \emph{enriched category}~\cite{kellyBasicConceptsEnriched1982} are isomorphic as categories.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Enrichment of a functor, {\cite[Def.~2.4]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}} \agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Functor.Base}{Enrichment}{}]
  Given categories \(C, D\) and enrichments \(EC, ED\) in a monoidal category \(V\) an \emph{enrichment} of a functor \(F \colon C → D\) in \(EC,ED\) is an external functorial action
  \(F_1' \colon 𝒱[EC.ℰ_{X,Y},ED.ℰ_{F_0 X, F_0 Y}]\), obeying external counterparts to preservation of identities and sequencing, such that \(F_1\) and \(F_1'\) \emph{agree}:
  \[∀ x, y : C_0,\, f : C[x,y].\ ED.\expi{F_1 f} = EC.\expi{f} ; F_1' \]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Locally Contractive Functors}
In the following, let \(V\) be a model of guarded recursive terms \cite[Def.~6.1]{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}. And categories \(C,D\) with enrichments \(EC, ED\) in \(V\).

\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~7.2]{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}}]
  A functor \(F \colon C → D\) with an enrichment \(FE\) in \(EC,ED\) is \emph{locally contractive} if it factors as the sequence of the enrichments
  \[CE \xrightarrow{\mathsf{next}} ▷CE \xrightarrow{F'} ED.\]
\end{definition}

We introduce the following corollary for the special case where one has a functor without a preexisting enrichment and wants its enrichment to be given by the sequence:

\begin{corollary}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.LocallyContractive}{}]
  \label{cor1}
A functor \(F \colon C → D\) is \emph{locally contractive} if it has as enrichment the sequence of enrichments \(CE \xrightarrow{\mathsf{next}} ▷CE \xrightarrow{F'} ED\), i.e.
\[∀ x, y : C_0,\, f : C[x,y].\ ED.\expi{F_1 f} = EC.\expi{f} ; \mathsf{next} ; F_1' \]
\end{corollary}

Surprisingly enough to us, as far as we are aware, noone in the literature has stated the following corollary:

\begin{corollary}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.LocallyContractive}{Hylo}{}]
  \label{cor2}
All coalgebras for a locally contractive functor \(F\) are recursive; likewise all algebras are corecursive.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  Note that in \eqref{fig:coalg-alg-morphism}, the functional\footnote{higher-order function} \(H(h) ≔ c ; h ; a\) has type \((F_0 I → F_0 O) → (I → O)\). Local contractivity of \(F\) gives us \(F_1' \colon ▷ (I → O) → (F_0I → F_0O) \). But guarded unique fixed points in \(V\) give us the rule \(\mathrm{Löb} \colon (▷ f → f) → f\). Thus we obtain \(h\) by \(\mathrm{Löb} (F_1' ; H)\)
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}
With Corollaries \ref{cor1} and \ref{cor2} we have the formalisation of our stated goal in \nameref{sec:intro}. To instantiate these to our setting of \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) for the well founded order \((A,<)\), we must choose: \begin{enumerate*}[label=\alph*), itemjoin=~]
\item a suitable model \(V\) of recursive terms to enrich in;
\item an enrichment of \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) (we can do better: more generally of \(𝒞^A\) for locally small \(𝒞\)) in \(V\).
\end{enumerate*}
For the former, following \cite{stepanenkoSolvingGuardedDomain2025} we chose presheaves over \((A,<)\).
For the latter, first note that \(𝒞^A\) is enriched in the monoidal category \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) with \(ℰ_{X,Y} ≔ \prod_{a ∈ A} 𝒞[X a, Y a]\). It remains to transport this enrichment over to \(\mathsf{Psh}(A,<)\), which we proceed to do in the following.
\paragraph{Change of Base}
Let \((M,*,I)\) and \((N,⊗,J)\) be monoidal categories.
\begin{definition}[Lax monoidal functor {\cite[Def.~2.1.1]{cruttwell2008normed}} \agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Monoidal.Functor}{LaxMonoidalFunctor}{}]
  A functor \(F \colon M → N\) is \emph{lax monoidal} if it has comparison arrows: \begin{itemize*}[itemjoin=~]
\item \(ε \colon N[J, F I]\), and
\item \(μ_{X,Y} \colon N[F x ⊗ F Y, F (x * y)]\) agreeing with the unitors and associators of \(M\) and \(N\).
\end{itemize*}
\begin{definition}[Preservation of Underlying Categories {\cite[Def.~3.6]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}} \agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Monoidal.Functor}{preservesUnderlyingCategories}{}]
  A lax monoidal functor \(F \colon M → N\) preserves underlying categories if \(\hat{ε}_A ≔ ε ; F_1– \colon M[I,A] → N[J, F A]\) is a bijection.
\end{definition}
Spoiler: This requirement is necessary for \(F^*(EC)\) to preserve \(C\) – \(F^*(EC).\expi{–} ≔ EC.\expi{–} ; \hat{ε}\).
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[Change of Base {\cite[Ex.~3.7]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}}\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.BaseChange.Base}{BaseChange}{}]
  Let \(EC\) be an enrichment of category \(C\) in \(M\). Let \(F\colon M → N\) be a lax monoidal functor which preserves underlying categories. We can define an enrichment \(F^*(EC)\) of \(C\) in \(N\) which we call the base-change of \(EC\) along \(F\).
\end{proposition}
Returning to our objective, it remains to define a lax monoidal functor which preserves underlying categories from \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) to \(\mathsf{Psh}(A,<)\).
The functor that does the job is the \emph{cofree presheaf functor}: \(U ⊣ \mathsf{Cofree}\), \(\mathsf{Cofree}(B)(x) ≔ \prod_y \mathsf{Set}[A(x,y),B(y)]\) (here \(A[x,y] = x ≤ y\)).
\begin{proposition}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Instances.Power}{Cᴬ-Psh-Enrichment}{}]
  \textsf{Cofree} is a lax monoidal functor preserving underlying categories.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  Its lax monoidal structure derives from the fact that as right adjoint it preserves limits. Preservation of underlying categories follows from \(\mathsf{Cofree}\) preserving terminal objects, followed by transposition along the adjunction.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Coda}
Thus, we find ourselves with with what we wanted from the outset: When working in the power category \(𝒞^A\) for some well founded order \(A,<\), as one does when defining intrinsically correct algorithms, one can express termination of an algorithm defined as a coalgebra-to-algorithm by proving that the functor \(F\) for which it is defined is locally contractive. This procedure is precisely the formal counterpart to what we layed out in \nameref{sec:intro} and elaborated in \nameref{sec:bridge}.
