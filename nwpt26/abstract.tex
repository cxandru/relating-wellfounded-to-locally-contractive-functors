The definition of recursive algorithms in verified total functional programming engenders two proof obligations: partial correctness and termination.
Often, partial correctness properties already entail facts from which termination follows.
We exploit such cases through a novel way to define recursive algorithms such that they are intrinsically correct and terminating.
Following the paradigm of structured recursion~\cite{hinzeConjugateHylomorphismsMother2015}, we separate the recursion behavior of divide-and-conquer algorithms from their recursive steps, which can be expressed categorically as (co)algebras for a functor.
We give conditions under which functors for intrinsically correct algorithms are \emph{locally contractive} \cite{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}, and show how termination of algorithms for such functors holds intrinsically.

\paragraph{Motivation}
Expressing recursive algorithms using coalgebra-to-algebra morphisms has a long history \cite{CaprettaUV06,jeanninWellfoundedCoalgebrasRevisited2017,taylor99,meijerFunctionalProgrammingBananas1991}.
The key idea is the following: In a base category \(𝒞\) modeling the types and functions of your functional programming language, choose an endofunctor \(F \colon 𝒞 → 𝒞\). We can think of this functor as describing a distribution; e.g.\ the functor \(B X ≔ 1 + X × A × X\) describes the distribution of either a base case \(* : 1\) or two inputs \(l , r : X\) and an output \(o : A\).
A recursive algorithm \(h\) can be seen as the following sequence of steps:
\begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=~]
\item \emph{divide} input into new input, with a distribution given by \(F_0\);
\item recur under \(F_1\);
\item \emph{combine} the results of the recursive invocations.
\end{enumerate*}
E.g.\ the recursive case of Quicksort \autoref{fig:qsort-recursive} (due to \cite{CaprettaUV06}); the general case is \autoref{fig:coalg-alg-morphism}.

A coalgebra-to-algebra morphism \(h\) is called a \emph{hylomorphism} if it is the unique coalgebra-to-algebra morphism making \eqref{eq:hylo} commute;  i.e.\ the equation \(h = c ; F h ; a\) has a unique solution and can be read as a \emph{definition}.
Note that in a non-total programming language, \texttt{h := c ; F h ; a} \emph{is already} a definition.
Our goal is to recover a similar definition scheme in a \emph{total} language.

This challenge becomes easier to tackle when we take partial correctness into account. Continuing with our Quicksort example, we home in on its coalgebra, \textsf{partition}. Its type, \(A^* → 1 + A^* × A^*\) is really too coarse. The partial correctness of \textsf{partition} entails, in particular, that the output \(B (A^*)\) value contain the same \emph{elements} as the input.
One approach to refine its type in order to capture this, due to \cite{AlexandruCRW25}, is to move to the setting of \emph{families} in \textsf{Set} indexed by the set \(ℳA\) of finite multisets of element type \(A\).
The refined \(B\) becomes \(\overline{B}\) (expressed for better comprehensibility in the slice category \(\mathsf{Set}/ℳA\) instead of in \(\mathsf{Fam}(ℳA) = \mathsf{Set}^{ℳA}\)) :
\[
  \overline{B} \begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    f
  \end{pmatrix}
  ≔
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 \\
    * ↦ \emptyset
  \end{pmatrix}
  +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \{(l,p,r) ∈ X × A × X \mid \dots\} \\
    (l, p, r) ↦ f(l) \uplus \{p\} \uplus f(r)
  \end{pmatrix}
\]
The refined type of \(\mathsf{partition}\) now encodes that the output triple contains the same elements as the input. 
\[
  \begin{tikzcd}[every arrow/.append style={mapsto},column sep=2cm]
 {ip} \arrow[r, "\mathsf{partition}"]\arrow[d, "\mathsf{toBag}"] & {(i_{≤ p}, p, i_{> p})} \arrow[d, "\mathsf{toBag} ⊗ \{–\} ⊗ \mathsf{toBag}"] \\
\mathsf{toBag}(i)\uplus \{p\} \arrow[r,equal] &  \mathsf{toBag}(i_{≤ p}) \uplus \{p\} \uplus \mathsf{toBag}(i_{> p})
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The interesting thing to note is the following: The multiset indices in functorial positions are smaller than the result index: \(f(l),f(r) < f(l) \uplus \{p\} \uplus f(r)\).

\paragraph{Sketch}\label{sec:intro}
We generalize to any functor \(F\) on a power category \(𝒞^A\) with \((A,<)\) a well founded ordering, where \enquote{the indices in functorial positions are smaller than the result index}. Such a functor gives solutions to \eqref{eq:hylo}: since the functorial indices are smaller, we construct \(h\) pointwise as an inhabitant of \(\ehom{𝒞^A}{I}{O}\) using well founded recursion: 
\[h_{\textcolor{red}{i}} ≔ c_i ; (F (h\textcolor{red}{|_{<i}}))_i ; a_i.\]
The key steps in making this sketch rigorous are the following : \begin{enumerate*}[label=\alph*), itemjoin=~]
\item Moving from Hom-\emph{sets} to Hom-\emph{objects} in some category which surfaces the requisite structure for constructing objects using well founded recursion
\item Defining what it means to be an alternative functorial action of \(F\) which takes morphisms \(\hom{\mathsf{Fam}(A)}{I}{O}\) restricted to smaller indices and produces a morphism \(\hom{𝒞^A}{F_0 I}{F_0 O}\) at the current index
\end{enumerate*}.
The tools we use to take these steps are \emph{enriched category theory} \cite{kellyBasicConceptsEnriched1982,weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026,cruttwell2008normed} and \emph{locally contractive functors} \cite{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}.

\paragraph{Enrichment}
\label{sec:enrichment}
To talk about an action of a functor \(F\) that isn't \(F_1\), we need the notion of:
\begin{definition}[Enrichment of a category \(C\) in a monoidal category \(V\), {\cite[Def.~2.2]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}}\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Base}{Enrichment}{}]
  An enrichment \(EC\) of a category \(C\) in a monoidal category \((V,⊗,I)\) consists of the following data:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item for every \(x, y \colon C\) the external Hom \(ℰ_{X,Y}\), an external identity morphism \& sequencing operation, and external counterparts to unitality \& associativity coherences;
  \item for every \(x, y \colon C\) a bijection \(\expi{-}\colon C[x,y] ≅ V[I,ℰ_{X,Y}]\) witnessing that \(C\) and the underlying category with objects \(C_0\) and homsets \(V[I,ℰ_{X,Y}]\) of \(EC\) viewed as an \emph{enriched category}~\cite{kellyBasicConceptsEnriched1982} are isomorphic as categories.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Enrichment of a functor, {\cite[Def.~2.4]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}} \agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Functor.Base}{Enrichment}{}]
  Given categories \(C, D\) and enrichments \((EC,\expi{-}), (ED,\expi{-}')\) in a monoidal category \(V\) an \emph{enrichment} of a functor \(F \colon C → D\) in \(EC,ED\) is an external functorial action
  \(F_1' \colon V[EC.ℰ_{X,Y},ED.ℰ_{F_0 X, F_0 Y}]\), obeying external counterparts to preservation of identities and sequencing, such that \(F_1\) and \(F_1'\) \emph{agree}:
  \[∀ x, y : C_0,\, f : C[x,y].\ \expi{F_1 f}' = \expi{f} ; F_1' \]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Locally Contractive Functors}
In the following, let \(V\) be a model of guarded recursive terms \cite[Def.~6.1]{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}. And categories \(C,D\) with enrichments \((EC,\expi{-}), (ED,\expi{-}')\) in \(V\).

\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~7.2]{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}}]
  A functor \(F \colon C → D\) with an enrichment \(FE\) in \(EC,ED\) is \emph{locally contractive} if it factors as the sequence of the enrichments
  \[EC \xrightarrow{\mathsf{next}} ▷EC \xrightarrow{F'} ED.\]
\end{definition}

We introduce the following corollary for the special case where one has a functor without a preexisting enrichment and wants its enrichment to be given by the sequence:

\begin{corollary}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.LocallyContractive}{isLocallyContractive}{}]
  \label{cor1}
A functor \(F \colon C → D\) is \emph{locally contractive} if it has as enrichment the sequence of enrichments \(EC \xrightarrow{\mathsf{next}} ▷EC \xrightarrow{F'} ED\), i.e. % TODO: explain enrichmentFor!
\[∀ x, y : C_0,\, f : C[x,y].\ \expi{F_1 f}' = \expi{f} ; \mathsf{next} ; F_1' \]
\end{corollary}

Surprisingly enough to us, as far as we are aware, noone in the literature has stated the following corollary:

\begin{corollary}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.LocallyContractive}{Hylo}{}]
  \label{cor2}
All coalgebras for a locally contractive functor \(F\) are recursive; likewise all algebras are corecursive.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  % TODO: fix add boundary constraints
  Note that in \eqref{eq:hylo}, the functional\footnote{higher-order function} \(H(h) ≔ c ; h ; a\) has type \((F_0 I → F_0 O) → (I → O)\). Local contractivity of \(F\) gives us \(F_1' \colon ▷ (I → O) → (F_0I → F_0O) \). But guarded unique fixed points in \(V\) give us the rule \(\mathrm{Löb} \colon (▷ f → f) → f\). Thus we obtain \(h\) by \(\mathrm{Löb} (F_1' ; H)\)
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}
With Corollaries \ref{cor1} and \ref{cor2} we have the formalisation of our stated goal in \nameref{sec:intro}. To instantiate these to our setting of \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) for the well founded order \((A,<)\), we must choose: \begin{enumerate*}[label=\alph*), itemjoin=~]
\item a suitable model \(V\) of recursive terms to enrich in;
\item an enrichment of \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) (we can do better: more generally of \(𝒞^A\) for locally small \(𝒞\)) in \(V\).
\end{enumerate*}
For the former, following \cite{stepanenkoSolvingGuardedDomain2025} we chose presheaves over \((A,<)\).
For the latter, first note that \(𝒞^A\) is enriched in the monoidal category \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) with \(ℰ_{X,Y} ≔ \prod_{a ∈ A} 𝒞[X a, Y a]\). It remains to transport this enrichment over to \(\mathsf{Psh}(A,<)\), which we proceed to do in the following.
\paragraph{Change of Base}
Let \((M,*,I)\) and \((N,⊗,J)\) be monoidal categories.
\begin{definition}[Lax monoidal functor {\cite[Def.~2.1.1]{cruttwell2008normed}} \agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Monoidal.Functor}{LaxMonoidalFunctor}{}]
  A functor \(F \colon M → N\) is \emph{lax monoidal} if it has comparison arrows: \begin{itemize*}[itemjoin=~]
\item \(ε \colon N[J, F I]\), and
\item \(μ_{X,Y} \colon N[F x ⊗ F Y, F (x * y)]\) agreeing with the unitors and associators of \(M\) and \(N\).
\end{itemize*}
\begin{definition}[Preservation of Underlying Categories {\cite[Def.~3.6]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}} \agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Monoidal.Functor}{preservesUnderlyingCategories}{}]
  A lax monoidal functor \(F \colon M → N\) preserves underlying categories if \(\hat{ε}_A ≔ ε ; F_1– \colon M[I,A] → N[J, F A]\) is a bijection.
\end{definition}
Spoiler: This requirement is necessary for \(F^*(EC)\) to preserve \(C\) – \(F^*(EC).\expi{–} ≔ EC.\expi{–} ; \hat{ε}\).
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[Change of Base {\cite[Ex.~3.7]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}}\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.BaseChange.Base}{BaseChange}{}]
  Let \(EC\) be an enrichment of category \(C\) in \(M\). Let \(F\colon M → N\) be a lax monoidal functor which preserves underlying categories. We can define an enrichment \(F^*(EC)\) of \(C\) in \(N\) which we call the base-change of \(EC\) along \(F\).
\end{proposition}
Returning to our objective, it remains to define a lax monoidal functor which preserves underlying categories from \(\mathsf{Set}^A\) to \(\mathsf{Psh}(A,<)\).
The functor that does the job is the \emph{cofree presheaf functor}: \(U ⊣ \mathsf{Cofree}\), \(\mathsf{Cofree}(B)(x) ≔ \prod_y \mathsf{Set}[A[x,y],B(y)]\) (here \(A[x,y] = x ≤ y\)).
\begin{proposition}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Instances.Power}{Cᴬ-Psh-Enrichment}{}]
  \textsf{Cofree} is a lax monoidal functor preserving underlying categories.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  Its lax monoidal structure derives from the fact that as right adjoint it preserves limits. Preservation of underlying categories follows from \(\mathsf{Cofree}\) preserving terminal objects, followed by transposition along the adjunction.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Coda}
Thus, we find ourselves with with what we wanted from the outset: When working in the power category \(𝒞^A\) for some well founded order \(A,<\), as one does when defining intrinsically correct algorithms, one can express termination of an algorithm defined as a coalgebra-to-algorithm by proving that the functor \(F\) for which it is defined is locally contractive. This procedure is precisely the formal counterpart to what we layed out in \nameref{sec:intro} and elaborated in \nameref{sec:bridge}. The incomplete Agda development accompanying this work is at \url{https://github.com/um-catlab/cubical-categorical-logic/tree/guarded-enriched}
