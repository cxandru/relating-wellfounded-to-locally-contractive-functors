The definition of recursive algorithms in verified total functional programming engenders two proof obligations: partial correctness and termination.
Often, partial correctness properties already entail facts from which termination follows.
We exploit such cases through a novel way to define recursive algorithms such that they are intrinsically correct and terminating.
Following the paradigm of structured recursion~\cite{hinzeConjugateHylomorphismsMother2015}, we separate the recursion behavior of divide-and-conquer algorithms from their recursive steps, which can be expressed categorically as (co)algebras for a functor.
We give conditions under which functors for intrinsically correct algorithms are \emph{locally contractive} \cite{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}, and show how termination of algorithms for such functors holds intrinsically.

\paragraph{Motivation}
Expressing recursive algorithms using coalgebra-to-algebra morphisms has a long history \cite{CaprettaUV06,jeanninWellfoundedCoalgebrasRevisited2017,taylor99,meijerFunctionalProgrammingBananas1991}.
The key idea is the following: In a base category \(𝒞\) modeling the types and functions of your functional programming language, choose an endofunctor \(F \colon 𝒞 → 𝒞\). We can think of this functor as describing a distribution; e.g.\ the functor \(B X ≔ 1 + X × A × X\) describes the distribution of either a base case \(* : 1\) or two inputs \(l , r : X\) and an output \(o : A\).
A recursive algorithm \(h\) can be seen as the following sequence of steps:
\begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=~]
\item \emph{divide} input into new input, with a distribution given by \(F_0\);
\item recur under \(F_1\);
\item \emph{combine} the results of the recursive invocations.
\end{enumerate*}
E.g.\ the recursive case of Quicksort \autoref{fig:qsort-recursive} (due to \cite{CaprettaUV06}); the general case is \autoref{fig:coalg-alg-morphism}.

A coalgebra-to-algebra morphism \(h\) is called a \emph{hylomorphism} if it is the unique coalgebra-to-algebra morphism making \eqref{eq:hylo} commute;  i.e.\ the equation \(h = c ; F h ; a\) has a unique solution and can be read as a \emph{definition}.
Note that in a non-total programming language, \texttt{h := c ; F h ; a} \emph{is already} a definition.
Our goal is to recover a similar definition scheme in a \emph{total} language.

This challenge becomes easier to tackle when we take partial correctness into account. Continuing with our Quicksort example, we home in on its coalgebra, \textsf{partition}. Its type, \(A^* → 1 + A^*× A × A^*\) is an underapproximation. The partial correctness of \textsf{partition} entails, in particular, that the output \(B (A^*)\) value contain the same \emph{elements} as the input. To capture this in the type, must refine it.
One approach, due to \cite{AlexandruCRW25}, is to move to the setting of \emph{families} in \textsf{Set} indexed by the set \(ℳA\) of finite multisets of element type \(A\).
The refined \(B\) becomes \(\overline{B}\) (expressed here in the slice category \(\mathsf{Set}/ℳA\) instead of in \(\mathsf{Fam}(ℳA) = \mathsf{Set}^{ℳA}\)) :
\[
  \overline{B} \begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    f
  \end{pmatrix}
  ≔
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 \\
    * ↦ \emptyset
  \end{pmatrix}
  +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \{(l,p,r) ∈ X × A × X \mid \dots\} \\
    (l, p, r) ↦ f(l) \uplus \{p\} \uplus f(r)
  \end{pmatrix}
\]
The refined type of \(\mathsf{partition}\) now encodes that the output triple contains the same elements as the input. 
\[
  \begin{tikzcd}[every arrow/.append style={mapsto},column sep=2cm]
 {ip} \arrow[r, "\mathsf{partition}"]\arrow[d, "\mathsf{toBag}"] & {(i_{≤ p}, p, i_{> p})} \arrow[d, "\mathsf{toBag} ⊗ \{–\} ⊗ \mathsf{toBag}"] \\
\mathsf{toBag}(i)\uplus \{p\} \arrow[r,equal] &  \mathsf{toBag}(i_{≤ p}) \uplus \{p\} \uplus \mathsf{toBag}(i_{> p})
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The thing of note is the following: The multiset indices in functorial positions are smaller than the result index: \(f(l),f(r) < f(l) \uplus \{p\} \uplus f(r)\). We proceed to sketch how functors with this property yield solutions to \eqref{eq:hylo}, yielding in this case an intrinsically correct and terminating implementation of Quicksort. We show that \(\overline{B}\) has this property in \nameref{sec:prestige}.

\paragraph{Sketch}\label{sec:intro}
We generalize to any functor \(F\) on a power category \(𝒞^A\) with \((A,<)\) a well founded ordering, where \enquote{the indices in functorial positions are smaller than the result index}. Such a functor gives solutions to \eqref{eq:hylo}: since the functorial indices are smaller, we construct \(h\) pointwise as an inhabitant of \(\ehom{𝒞^A}{I}{O}\) using well founded recursion: 
\[h_{\textcolor{red}{i}} ≔ c_i ; (F (h\textcolor{red}{|_{<i}}))_i ; a_i.\]
The key steps in making this sketch rigorous are the following : \begin{enumerate*}[label=\alph*), itemjoin=~]
\item Moving from Hom-\emph{sets} to Hom-\emph{objects} in some category which surfaces the requisite structure for constructing objects using well founded recursion
\item Defining what it means to be an alternative functorial action of \(F\) which takes morphisms \(\hom{\mathsf{Fam}(A)}{I}{O}\) restricted to smaller indices and produces a morphism \(\hom{𝒞^A}{F_0 I}{F_0 O}\) at the current index
\end{enumerate*}.
The tools we use to take these steps are \emph{enriched category theory} \cite{kellyBasicConceptsEnriched1982,cruttwell2008normed} and \emph{locally contractive functors} \cite{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}.

\begin{remark}
  We use both the notion of \emph{enriched categories} and that of \emph{enrichment}~\cite{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026}. We make this distinction because we work with lax monoidal functors which do not necessarily preserve underlying categories (\autoref{def:laxmon}). Because of this we differentiate at times between categories \(C\) and their set of objects \(C_0\) and functors \(F\) and their action on objects \(F_0\).
\end{remark}
We fix a monoidal category \((V,⊗,𝟙)\).
\begin{definition}
  An \emph{enriched category} \(C'\) for a set \(C_0\) of objects has for every \(X, Y \colon C_0\) the internal Hom \((\ihom{X}{Y}) \colon V\).
  We denote with \(Γ X ≔ \hom{V}{𝟙}{X}; Γ \colon V → \mathsf{Set}\) the global element functor.
  \(C'\) has an \emph{underlying} plain (i.e. \(\mathsf{Set}\)-enriched) category \((Γ^*C', \ihom[']{}{})\) with \(\ihom[']{X}{Y} ≔ \glob{V}{𝟙}{\ihom{X}{Y}}\).
  An \emph{enrichment} of a category \(C\) is an enriched category for \(C_0\), together with an isomorphism of categories \(C ≅ Γ^*C'\) with, in particular, \(\expi{-}\colon \hom{C}{X}{Y} ≅ \glob{V}{𝟙}{\ihom{X}{Y}}\).%, witnessing the agreement of external and internal homs.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Instances.Power}{Cᴬ-Enrichment}{}]\label{ex:fam}
  \(\mathsf{Fam}(A)\) is monoidal, inheriting that structure pointwise from \(\mathsf{Set}\). In particular, \(𝟙 = ∀ i ∈ A.\, 𝟙_{\mathsf{Set}} ≅ A\).
  \(𝒞^A\) is enriched in \(\mathsf{Fam}(A)\) with \((\ihom{X}{Y}) i = \hom{\mathsf{Set}}{X i }{Y i}\) and \(\expi{-}\colon \hom{\mathsf{Set}^{A}}{X }{Y} = ∀ i ∈ A.\, \hom{\mathsf{Set}}{X i }{Y i} ≅ ∀ i ∈ A.\, \hom{\mathsf{Set}}{𝟙_{\mathsf{Set}}}{\hom{\mathsf{Set}}{X i }{Y i}} = \hom{\mathsf{Set}^{A}}{𝟙}{\ihom{X}{Y}}\).
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Monoidal.Guarded}{GuardedModel}{}]
  A \emph{model of guarded recursive terms}~\cite[Def.~6.1]{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012} for a monoidal category \(E\) has a strong monoidal endofunctor \(▸ \colon E → E\), a natural transformation \(\mathsf{next} \colon \hom{[E,E]}{\mathsf{Id}}{▸}\), and a fixpoint principle called \emph{Löb induction}: \(∀ H \colon \hom{E}{▸ P}{P}.\ ∃!\, \mathsf{Löb}H \colon ΓP.\, \mathsf{Löb}H = \mathsf{Löb}H; \mathsf{next}; H\).
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Direct.Guarded.Presheaf}{pshGuarded}{}]\label{ex:pshguard}
  \(\mathsf{PSh}(A,<)\) is a model of guarded recursive terms~\cite{stepanenkoSolvingGuardedDomain2025}. Concretely, we have \(▸ P i = ∀ j < i.\, P j\); \(\mathsf{next}\) is given by the presheaf's restriction maps; instantiating the type of \(\mathsf{Löb}\) we see that it corresponds precisely to well founded recursion: \((∀ i .\, (∀ j < i.\, P j) → P i) → ∀ i .\, P i\).
\end{example}

We fix monoidal categories \((M,*,I)\) and \((N,⊗,J)\).
\begin{definition}\label{def:laxmon}
  A \emph{lax monoidal functor} \(F \colon M → N\) has comparison arrows \(ε \colon \hom{N}{J}{F I}\) and \(μ_{X,Y} \colon \hom{N}{F X ⊗ F Y}{F (X * Y)}\). It \emph{preserves underlying categories}~\cite[Def.~3.6]{weideUnivalentEnrichedCategories2026} if \(\hat{ε}_X ≔ ε ; F_1– \colon \hom{\mathsf{Set}}{\ehom{M}{I}{X}}{\ehom{N}{J}{F X}}\) is a bijection.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
  Let \((C',\ihom{}{})\) be an \(M\)-enriched category for \(C_0\). Let \(F\colon M → N\) be a lax monoidal functor. There is an \(N\)-enriched category \((F^*C',\ihom[']{}{})\) for \(C_0\) called \emph{the base-change of \(C\) along \(F\)} with \(\ihom[']{X}{Y} ≔ F (\ihom{X}{Y})\). If \(F\) additionally preserves underlying categories, it induces a change of base of \emph{enrichment}. For \((EC,\expi{-})\) set \((F^*(EC),\expi{-};\hat{ε})\).
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}
  The \emph{cofree presheaf functor} \(\mathsf{Cofree} \colon  \mathsf{Fam}(A) → \mathsf{PSh}(A,<)\) is the right adjoint to the forgetful functor \(U \colon \mathsf{PSh}(A,<) → \mathsf{Fam}(A)\) with \(\mathsf{Cofree}\, X i = ∀ j ≤ i. \, X i\). It is lax monoidal and preserves underlying categories.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.Instances.Power}{Cᴬ-Psh-Enrichment}{}]\label{ex:fampsh}
\(𝒞^A\) is enriched in \(\mathsf{PSh}(A,<)\), with the enrichment given by change-of-base of its enrichment in \(\mathsf{Fam}(A)\) (\autoref{ex:fam}) along \(\mathsf{Cofree}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}
  An \emph{enriched functor} between enriched categories \((C',\ihom{}{})\), \((D',\ihom[']{}{})\) for \(C_0,D_0\), and a map \(F_0 \colon \hom{\mathsf{Set}}{C_0}{D_0}\), has an internal functorial action \(F_1' \colon \hom{V}{\ihom{X}{Y}}{\ihom[']{F_0 X}{F_0 Y}'}\).
  Given categories \(C, D\) with enrichments \((\expi{-})\) and \((\expi{-}')\), an \emph{enrichment} of a functor \(F \colon C → D\) is an enriched functor for \(F_0\) such that \(F_1\) and \(F_1'\) \emph{agree}: \(∀ f : \hom{C}{X}{Y}.\ \expi{F_1 f}' = \expi{f} ; F_1'\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.LocallyContractive}{isLocallyContractive}{}]
  Fix \(V\) a model of guarded recursive terms, categories \(C,D\) with \(V\)-enrichments \((EC,\expi{-}), (ED,\expi{-}')\).
  A functor \(F \colon C → D\) is \emph{locally contractive}~\cite[Def.~6.1]{birkedalFirstStepsSynthetic2012}, if there exists an enriched functor \(F_1' \colon ▸^*(EC) → ED\) for \(F_0\) such that the sequence \(EC \midarrow{\mathsf{next}} ▸^*(EC) \midarrow{F'} ED\) is an enrichment of \(F\), i.e.
  \(∀ f \colon \hom{C}{X}{Y}.\ \expi{F_1 f}' = \expi{f} ; \mathsf{next} ; F_1'\)
\end{definition}
\begin{example}
  For \(\mathsf{PSh}(A,<)\) as model of guarded recursive terms (\autoref{ex:pshguard}), we have \(\expi{f};\mathsf{next}\ i = \expi{f} \mid_{<i}\) So \enquote{\(\expi{F_1 f} = \expi{f};\mathsf{next};F_1'\)} says \enquote{\(F_1'\) is a way to apply \(F\) to Homs pointwise restricted to lower indices}
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[\agdaref{Cubical.Categories.Enriched.Enrichment.LocallyContractive}{Hylo}{}]
  \label{thm}
Locally contractive functors give rise to hylomorphisms.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Note that in \eqref{eq:hylo}, the functional\footnote{higher-order function} \(H(f) ≔ c ; f ; a\) has type \(\hom{V}{(\ihom{F_0 I}{F_0 O})}{(\ihom{I}{O})}\). Local contractivity of \(F\) gives us \(F_1' \colon \hom{V}{▸ (\ihom{I}{O})}{(\ihom{F_0 I}{F_0 O})}\). Putting them together we get \(▸ (\ihom{I}{O}) \midarrow{F_1'} (\ihom{F_0 I}{F_0 O}) \midarrow{H} (\ihom{I}{O})\). Applying \(\mathsf{Löb} \colon (▸ (\ihom{I}{O}) → (\ihom{I}{O})) → \glob{V}{𝟙}{\ihom{I}{O}}\) gives us \(\expi{\mathsf{Löb} (F_1' ; H)} \colon (\hom{C}{I}{O})\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph[The Prestige]{The Prestige\footnote{\url{https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/91029-every-great-magic-trick-consists-of-three-parts-or-acts\#:~:text="The Prestige"}}}
\label{sec:prestige}
Enriching the category of families in presheaves (\autoref{ex:fampsh}) allows us to express what it means for the Quicksort functor \(\overline{B}\colon \mathsf{Fam}(ℳA) → \mathsf{Fam}(ℳA)\) to be locally contractive. We state its contractive action \(\overline{B}' \colon \hom{\mathsf{Fam}(A)}{▸ (\ihom{X}{Y})}{(\ihom{\overline{B}X}{\overline{B}Y})}\) below. What allows us to define it is precisely that the indices in functorial positions are smaller than in the result.
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{alignedat}{9}
    &\overline{B}' \;&&\mathrlap{\colon ∀ i .\, \hom{𝒞}{(∀ j < i.\, \hom{𝒞}{X j}{Y j})}{(\hom{𝒞}{\overline{B}X i}{\overline{B}Y i})}} \\
    &\overline{B}'   &&(i = ∅)\ &&f\ &&\ * &&≔ * \\
    &\overline{B}'   &&(i = j \uplus \{p\} \uplus k)\ &&f &&(l ∈ X j, p , r ∈ X k) &&≔(f l , p , f r)
  \end{alignedat}
\end{equation*}
Consequently, we instantiate \autoref{thm} to obtain that Quicksort intrinsically terminates as a coalgebra-to-algebra morphism for \(\overline{B}\).

\paragraph{Related Work}
Our approach of showing functors for intrinsically correct algorithms to be locally contractive to intrinsically obtain termination is quite similar to the the approach of showing such functors to be \emph{well founded}~\cite{alexandruIntrinsicallyCorrectAlgorithms2026}, also obtaining termination intrinsically. We conjecture that well founded functors are locally contractive, which would make our result stronger. Exploring the connection fully remains future work.

\paragraph{Conclusion}
We began with intrinsically correct algorithms expressed as coalgebra-to-algebra morphisms in a category \(𝒞^A\) for some functor \(F\). If \(A\) can be equipped with a well founded ordering, \(𝒞^A\) can be enriched in \(\mathsf{PSh}(A,<)\) and, if \(F\) is locally contractive for this enrichment, algorithms for \(F\) intrinsically terminate. We demonstrated the technique on Quicksort.
